A scissor lift that is sized for its raised position stalls at the bottom. When the arms lie almost flat, the actuator must push with several times the weight of the load, and a lift drawn with all its joints as plain pins cannot move at all. Both facts come from the geometry of the crossed arms. In this lesson you model a single-stage scissor lift in FreeCAD from one arm that is used twice, with every size taken from a spreadsheet. You calculate its height and its actuator force from one angle, and you see why two of its joints must slide. #FreeCAD #ScissorLift #VerticalMotion #RepeatingGeometry

:::tip[Simulate before you build]
Before designing the 3D model, try your link lengths in the [Scissor Lift Mechanism Simulator](/product-development/scissor-lift-mechanism-simulator/) to verify force requirements, check stability at different heights, and compare actuator types. For structured kinematic and force experiments with Python analysis, see the [Scissor Lift Experiments](/education/mechanism-design-simulation/scissor-lift-experiments).
:::

## Engineering Context: Why This Mechanism Matters

<InArticleAd />


A scissor lift raises a platform straight up with crossed arms that open and close as scissors do. Car jacks, work platforms, lifting tables and loading docks use it, because it is short when lowered and needs no tall guide.

<YouTube videoId="LKaQgUK1B2M" title="Scissor Lift Mechanism Design in FreeCAD" />

### The Engineering Problem

A platform must rise straight up from a low rest position, and the actuator has only a short horizontal travel at the base. How high does the platform go for a given arm, and how hard must the actuator push?

## Mechanism Fundamentals

<InArticleAd />


### Components and Motion

One stage is two equal arms that cross at a center pin, between a fixed base and a top platform. Each arm has three pin holes: one at each end and one in the middle. On each platform one arm end is held by a fixed pin and the other must slide, or nothing can move:

```text
  fixed pin    sliding pin
      ●───────────○  ◄─►     top platform, rises
       ╲         ╱
        ╲       ╱
         ╲     ╱
          ╲   ╱
            ●                center pin
          ╱   ╲
         ╱     ╲
        ╱       ╲
       ╱         ╲
      ●───────────○  ◄─►     base, fixed
  fixed pin    sliding pin
```

Three numbers describe the motion:

- $L$ is the arm length between its two end pins, in mm.
- $\theta$ is the angle of the arms from the horizontal.
- $n$ is the number of stages, one pair of crossed arms above another.

Each arm is the long side of a right triangle. Its upright side is the height of one stage and its flat side is the distance between the two base pins. So the platform height $h$ and the base spread $x$ are:

$$h = n \, L \sin\theta \qquad x = L \cos\theta$$

The lift works between a lowest angle of 15° to 20° and a highest angle of 60° to 70°. Above 70° the base pins stand close together under a tall platform, and the lift is no longer stable.

A horizontal actuator at the base pulls the two base pins together. When the arms lie almost flat, a small pull gives a large rise. The work put in equals the work done on the load $W$, so a large rise for a small pull needs a large force $F$:

$$F = \frac{W \, n}{\tan\theta}$$

This is for arms without weight and pins without friction. It is why a scissor jack is hardest to turn at the start.

### Dimensions for this lesson

The lesson builds one stage ($n = 1$) with arms of $L = 200 \text{ mm}$, and places the base pins 160 mm apart in the start position:

1. Arm angle: $\cos\theta = 160 / 200 = 0.80$, so $\theta = 36.9^\circ$ ✅
2. Height at the start: $h = 200 \times \sin 36.9^\circ = 200 \times 0.60 = 120 \text{ mm}$ ✅
3. Lowest and highest: $200 \times \sin 15^\circ = 51.8 \text{ mm}$ and $200 \times \sin 70^\circ = 187.9 \text{ mm}$ ✅
4. Actuator force at the start: $F = W / \tan 36.9^\circ = 1.33\,W$. At 15° it is $W / \tan 15^\circ = 3.73\,W$ ✅

## Part 1: Design Strategy and Planning

<InArticleAd />


### Understanding Scissor Geometry

Both arms of a stage are the same part. If you model two arms separately, a later change to one is easily forgotten in the other, and then the center pins no longer meet. So you model **one** arm and place it in the assembly twice. A change to the arm then reaches every copy.

The center hole must stay exactly in the middle of the arm at every arm length, or the platform tilts as it rises. A typed distance of 100 mm is right only while the arm is 200 mm long. A midpoint constraint is right at every length.

The order of work: spreadsheet, arm, base, top platform, assembly, drawing, and then tests of the parameters.

## Part 3: Creating the Scissor Arm

<InArticleAd />


### Design Intent

The arm is a flat bar with three pin holes: one at each end, for the base and the top platform, and one in the middle, where the two arms cross. The sketch starts with a **centerline**, a line of length ArmLength that makes no solid. The three holes and the outline take their positions from this line, so one dimension places everything.

A sketch is exact only when its **constraints** (rules such as "this point is on that line" or "this distance is 200 mm") fix every point. Until then the solver reports **degrees of freedom**, the ways in which the sketch can still move. A sketch with degrees of freedom left can change shape without warning when a value changes.

### Creating the Arm Body

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Rectangle Profile">

    **Draw the structural beam:**

    <Steps>

    1. **Rectangle tool**

    2. Draw a rectangle that encompasses all three circles

    3. Should extend past the leftmost and rightmost circles

    4. Press **Escape**

    </Steps>

    Don't worry about exact positioning yet. Constraints will fix it!

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Symmetric Constraint">

    **Center the rectangle about centerline:**

    <Steps>

    1. Click **Symmetric constraint** tool

    2. Click the **top edge** of the rectangle

    3. Click the **bottom edge** of the rectangle

    4. Click the **horizontal centerline** (construction line)

    5. Press **Escape**

    </Steps>

    The symmetric constraint keeps the two long edges at equal distances from the centerline. When ArmWidth changes, the bar grows equally on both sides and the holes stay in the middle.

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Width Dimension">

    **Set the parametric width:**

    <Steps>

    1. **Distance constraint** tool

    2. Click the **top edge** of rectangle

    3. Click the **bottom edge** of rectangle

    4. ƒx button

    5. Type: `Spreadsheet.ArmWidth`

    6. Enter

    </Steps>

    Arm width is now 30mm and parametric!

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Length Lock">

    **The rectangle length still needs two constraints!**

    The symmetric and width constraints fix the rectangle only in the vertical direction. The solver still shows 2 degrees of freedom: the positions of the left and right edges. These edges must lie beyond the end holes, otherwise the holes cut through the ends of the arm.

    To remove the 2 degrees of freedom, add:

    - **Distance:** Left hole center → rectangle left edge = `Spreadsheet.ArmWidth / 2`
    - **Distance:** Right hole center → rectangle right edge = `Spreadsheet.ArmWidth / 2`

    The arm is now ArmLength + ArmWidth long overall (230 mm), with 200 mm between the end holes.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Part 6: Assembly - Single Stage

<InArticleAd />


An assembly joins the parts with constraints between parts, so that you can drag the platform and watch it rise. The arm is placed twice: once rising from left to right, and once from right to left.

:::caution[Which Assembly workbench these steps use]
These steps were written for FreeCAD 0.21, which has no assembly workbench of its own: you install one from **Tools → Addon manager**. The lesson names Assembly4 and A2plus, but the constraint names it uses (Fixed, Axial Align, point-on-line) are nearest to the **Assembly3** add-on (Axial Alignment, Points on Line). A2plus calls them axisCoincident and pointOnLine, and Assembly4 has no constraints of this kind. FreeCAD 1.0 and later have a built-in Assembly workbench with other names: ground the base where this lesson says Fixed, and use a **Revolute** joint where it says Axial Align.
:::

### Assembly Strategy

The base is fixed. The first arm rises from the left base pin to the right end of the top platform. The second arm rises from the right end of the base to the left pin of the top platform. The two arms share the center pin.

:::caution[One end of each platform must slide]
Count the joints before you assemble. There are 4 links (base, two arms, top platform). If all five connections are fixed pin joints, the mobility is $3(4-1) - 2(5) = -1$: the assembly is a rigid frame and cannot lift. With the holes 160 mm apart and 200 mm arms it fits only at one height, $\sqrt{200^2 - 160^2} = 120 \text{ mm}$.

A real scissor lift lets one end of each platform slide (a roller or a pin in a horizontal slot). Here the two right-hand connections slide: the second arm on the base, and the first arm on the top platform. A pin in a slot removes one degree of freedom, not two, so the mobility becomes $3(4-1) - 2(3) - 1(2) = 1$. In the assembly, do not use Axial Align for these two connections. Use a constraint that keeps the arm hole axis on the line through the two platform holes (a point-on-line or slot type constraint), and in a part you intend to build, replace the right-hand hole of each platform with a horizontal slot.
:::

### Creating the Assembly

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Setup">

    <Steps>

    1. **Switch to Assembly workbench**

       Use workbench dropdown (Assembly4 or A2plus recommended)

    2. **Create new assembly**

       Assembly → Create Assembly

    3. **Add parts**

       You need to add:
       - BasePlatform (once)
       - ScissorArm (twice - same part, two instances!)
       - TopPlatform (once)

       Use "Add Part" or drag from tree, adding ScissorArm twice

    </Steps>

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Fixing Base">

    <Steps>

    1. Select **BasePlatform** in assembly tree

    2. Click **Fixed constraint** button

    3. Base is now locked in place (ground link)

    </Steps>

    :::note[Why Fix the Base?]
    Every mechanism needs a ground reference. The base platform provides this fixed foundation from which all vertical motion is measured.
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="First Arm Position">

    **Position the left-rising arm:**

    <Steps>

    1. Select the **left end hole axis** of the first ScissorArm

    2. Select the **left pin hole axis** of the BasePlatform

    3. Click **Axial Align constraint**

    </Steps>

    The first arm's left end is now connected to the base!

    :::tip[Naming Parts]
    Rename your ScissorArm instances in the assembly to "ScissorArm_Left" and "ScissorArm_Right" to avoid confusion!
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Second Arm Position">

    **Position the right-rising arm:**

    <Steps>

    1. Select the **left end hole axis** of the second ScissorArm

    2. Select the **right pin hole axis** of the BasePlatform

    3. Click **Axial Align constraint** to place the arm, then replace it with the sliding (point-on-line) constraint described in the caution above, so this end can move along the base

    </Steps>

    The second arm's left end connects to the right side of the base!

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Center Crossing">

    <Steps>

    1. Select the **center hole axis** of first ScissorArm

    2. Select the **center hole axis** of second ScissorArm

    3. Click **Axial Align constraint**

    </Steps>

    The arms now cross and pivot on each other.

    :::caution[Assembly Critical Point]
    Without this center constraint the two arms move independently. With it they act as a scissor and amplify the motion.
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Top Platform">

    **Connect to the top platform:**

    <Steps>

    1. First arm's **right end hole** → TopPlatform **right pin hole**
       - Sliding (point-on-line) constraint, so this end can move along the platform

    2. Second arm's **right end hole** → TopPlatform **left pin hole**
       - Axial Align constraint

    </Steps>

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Testing Motion">

    **Test the scissor action:**

    Try dragging the top platform or an arm in the assembly view:
    - Arms should rotate about their pivots
    - Center crossing should move
    - Top platform should rise and fall!
    - Motion should be smooth and coordinated

    :::tip[Troubleshooting Motion]
    **If mechanism won't move:**
    - Check that only BasePlatform is Fixed
    - All pin joints should be Axial Align (not Fixed!)
    - Remove any redundant constraints
    - Verify you have 4 arm-to-platform connections + 1 crossing connection, and that the two right-hand platform connections slide (five fixed pins lock the lift at 120 mm)
    :::

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Common Issues and Solutions

<InArticleAd />


<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Sketch Issues">

    **"Center hole not at midpoint"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - Use **Midpoint constraint**, not approximate positioning
    - Select circle center → Select construction line
    - Apply Midpoint constraint from toolbar
    - Verify constraint icon appears on line
    :::

    **"Rectangle not symmetric"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - Use **Symmetric constraint** on edges
    - Select top edge, bottom edge, then centerline
    - Order matters: two objects, then reference line
    - Check that centerline is construction geometry
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Parametric Issues">

    **"PinRadius doesn't update with PinDiameter"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - Check cell B6 contains formula: `=B5/2`
    - Verify alias is on B6, not B5
    - Delete and recreate the formula if needed
    - Recompute after spreadsheet changes
    :::

    **"Arms don't scale uniformly"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - Verify all dimensions use formulas (ƒx icon visible)
    - Check for hardcoded values that should be parametric
    - Ensure both arm instances reference same source part
    - Try "Mark to Recompute" on body, then Ctrl+R
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Assembly Issues">

    **"Arms don't cross properly"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - Verify center hole exists on both arms
    - Check Axial Align constraint on center holes
    - Ensure arms are oriented correctly (one rises left, one rises right)
    - May need to flip one arm orientation
    :::

    **"Platform holes don't match arm holes"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - All holes must use same `Spreadsheet.PinRadius`
    - Check spacing matches collapsed geometry
    - For single stage: platform holes 80 mm from center (the sliding ends move away from this position as the lift rises or falls)
    - Verify all radius constraints reference parameter
    :::

    **"Can't add ScissorArm twice"**

    :::caution[Solution]
    - Use assembly workbench "Add Part" button twice
    - Or right-click body → "Duplicate Object" before assembly
    - Assembly4: Use "Insert Link" for same part multiple times
    - A2plus: Import the same part file twice
    :::

</TabItem>
</Tabs>
