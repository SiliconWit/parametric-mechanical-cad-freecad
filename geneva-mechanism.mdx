## Engineering Context: Why This Mechanism Matters

<InArticleAd />


The Geneva mechanism (also called the Maltese cross mechanism) gives intermittent rotary motion: the driver turns continuously, and the driven wheel steps to exact positions and is locked still between steps.

The Geneva mechanism appears everywhere precise indexing is needed:

:::note[Common Applications]
- **Film projectors** - Advancing film frame-by-frame with precise positioning
- **Movie cameras** - Frame advance between exposures
- **Automatic machine tool changers** - Rotating tool turrets to exact positions
- **Conveyor indexing systems** - Precise product positioning

- **Packaging machinery** - Precise container positioning for filling operations

### The Engineering Problem

> **Design Challenge:** Given continuous rotational input, how do we create intermittent, precise angular output with exact dwell periods and self-locking to prevent drift between indexes?

## Mechanism Fundamentals

<InArticleAd />


### Components and Motion

A Geneva mechanism consists of three key elements working together:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Components">

    1. **Driver (crank):** turns continuously and carries a pin and a locking arc on its periphery.
    2. **Driven wheel (Geneva wheel):** has radial slots (typically 4, 5, 6 or 8). It turns one step when the pin is in a slot and stays still during the dwell.
    3. **Locking arc:** a circular arc on the driver that bears on the periphery of the driven wheel and stops it from turning during the dwell.

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Operation Phases">

    1. **Dwell (locked):** the locking arc bears on the periphery of the driven wheel, so the wheel stays still while the driver turns.
    2. **Index (motion):** the pin enters a slot and turns the wheel to the next position.
    3. **Return to dwell:** the pin leaves the slot and the locking arc holds the wheel again.

    :::tip[Engineering Insight]
    Without the locking arc, the driven wheel could drift or vibrate during the dwell and lose its position. This self-locking is the advantage of a Geneva mechanism over a simple indexing cam.
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Key Parameters">

    <Card title="⚙️ Critical Design Parameters" icon="setting">

    **Number of slots ($n$)**
    - Typically 4, 5, 6, or 8
    - Determines index angle: $\theta = 360° / n$
    - More slots = smaller index steps

    **Driver radius ($R_d$)**
    - Distance from driver center to pin center
    - Calculated: $R_d = C \times \sin(180° / n)$, where $C$ is the center distance

    **Driven wheel radius ($R_g$)**
    - Distance from center to slot entry
    - Calculated: $R_g = R_d / \tan(180° / n) = C \times \cos(180° / n)$

    **Dwell ratio**
    - Fraction of driver rotation where driven wheel is stationary
    - For $n$ slots: Dwell $= (n + 2) / (2n)$ of full rotation

    </Card>

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Mathematical Relationships

For an external Geneva mechanism with n slots:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Geometric Formula">

    **Driven wheel radius calculation:**

    $$R_g = \frac{R_d}{\tan\left(\frac{180°}{n}\right)}$$

    **Where:**
    - $R_g$ = Geneva wheel radius (center to slot entry)
    - $R_d$ = Driver radius (center to pin), with $R_d = C \sin(180°/n)$ for center distance $C$
    - $n$ = Number of slots

    These two radii make the pin enter and leave each slot along the slot direction, with no impact: at entry the crank is at a right angle to the slot, so $R_d^2 + R_g^2 = C^2$.

    **Slot spacing:**

    $$\text{Slot center angle} = \frac{360°}{n}$$

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Timing Analysis">

    **Index angle per step:**

    $$\theta = \frac{360°}{n}$$

    **Dwell angle:**

    $$\text{Dwell} = 180° + \frac{360°}{n} = \frac{360° \times (n + 2)}{2n}$$

    **Index duration:**

    $$\text{Index} = 180° - \frac{360°}{n}$$

    :::note[Example: 6-Slot Geneva]
    - Index angle: 360° / 6 = 60° per step
    - Dwell: 180° + 60° = 240° of driver rotation
    - Index: 180° - 60° = 120° of driver rotation
    - The driven wheel is stationary for 2/3 of each cycle!
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Common Configurations">

    | Slots | Index Angle | Dwell Ratio | Application |
    |-------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
    | **4-slot** | 90° | 75% (270°) | Film projectors |
    | **6-slot** | 60° | 67% (240°) | Machine tool changers |
    | **8-slot** | 45° | 62.5% (225°) | High-frequency indexing |

    We'll build a **6-slot Geneva** for balanced performance and clear learning.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Part 2: Parametric Design Strategy

<InArticleAd />


Change the number of slots of a Geneva, and every other dimension must be calculated again, so formulas in the spreadsheet drive the whole model.

### Our Design Approach

<Card title="🎯 Multi-Parameter Control Philosophy" icon="rocket">

We'll control the **entire Geneva mechanism** with key parameters:

**NumSlots** = 6 (number of slots)
**DriverCenterDistance** = 80 mm (between wheel centers)
**PinDiameter** = 10 mm

From these, FreeCAD will **automatically calculate**:
- Slot angle spacing (60°)
- Driven wheel radius (geometric formula)
- Index angle per step
- Slot width (with clearance)

</Card>

### Our Geneva Configuration

We'll design a **6-slot external Geneva mechanism**:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Configuration">

### Drawing the Driver Profile

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Main Disk">

    **Draw the outer circle:**

    <Steps>
    1. Click **Circle tool** (or press G, C)
    2. Click at the **origin** (white/yellow dot at center)
    3. Move mouse outward and click to complete circle
    4. Press **Escape**

    5. **Radius constraint:**
       - Click the circle
       - Click **ƒx** and type: `Spreadsheet.DriverRadius + 10` (50 mm here, so the disk stays larger than the pin circle when DriverRadius changes)
       - Press **Enter**
    </Steps>

    This creates the main driver body.

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Shaft Hole">

    **Draw the center shaft hole:**

    <Steps>
    1. **Circle tool** again (press G, C)
    2. Click at **origin**
    3. Draw a smaller circle inside
    4. Press **Escape**

    5. **Make it concentric:**
       - Centers already coincident (both at origin)

    6. **Parametric radius:**
       - **Radius constraint** tool
       - Click the inner circle
       - Click **ƒx** button
       - Type: `Spreadsheet.ShaftRadius`
       - Press **Enter**
    </Steps>

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Driving Pin">

    **Draw the hole for the driving pin.** A circle inside the disk is cut out by the Pad, so this circle makes a hole. It marks where the pin goes; the pin itself is padded after the locking disc and fills this hole.

    <Steps>
    1. **Circle tool** (press G, C)
    2. Click somewhere to the **right** of center (on horizontal axis)
    3. Draw a small circle
    4. Press **Escape**

    5. **Constrain pin position horizontally:**
       - Click **Horizontal constraint** tool
       - Click the **pin circle center**
       - Click the **origin**
       - This locks pin on horizontal axis!

    6. **Parametric distance from center:**
       - **Distance constraint** tool
       - Click **origin point**
       - Click **pin circle center**
       - Click **ƒx** button
       - Type: `Spreadsheet.DriverRadius`
       - Press **Enter**

    7. **Parametric pin radius:**
       - **Radius constraint** tool
       - Click the pin circle
       - Click **ƒx** button
       - Type: `Spreadsheet.PinRadius`
       - Press **Enter**
    </Steps>

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Check Constraints">

    **Verify the sketch:**

    Look at "Solver Messages" panel:
    - Should say **"Fully constrained"**
    - 3 circles: outer disk, shaft hole, driving pin
    - All dimensions linked to spreadsheet

    :::tip[Locking Arc Note]
    The driver disk (radius 50 mm) and the Geneva wheel (radius 69 mm) overlap at a center distance of 80 mm, so this disk sits on a level below the wheel and does not touch it. It does not lock the wheel. The lock is a smaller locking disc on top of this disk, at the level of the wheel, which sits in matching hollows cut between the slots of the wheel. A cut-away on the pin side of the locking disc lets the wheel turn during the index. You add both after the pad below.
    :::

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Creating 3D: Pad Operation

<Steps>

1. **Close the sketch**

   Click the **Close** button in the toolbar

2. **Pad the profile**

   - Select the sketch in the tree (if not selected)
   - Click **Pad** tool in Part Design toolbar
   - In the Pad panel (left side):
     - Type: **Dimension**
     - Length: Click **ƒx** button
     - Type: `Spreadsheet.WheelThickness`
     - Click **OK**

</Steps>

Press **V** then **F** to fit the view to the 3D driver wheel.

### Adding the Locking Disc and the Pin

The pin circle in the first sketch made a hole, not a pin. These steps add the locking disc, its cut-away and a pin that stands up into the level of the wheel. Each new sketch goes on the **top face** of the solid below it (select the face, then **Create Sketch**).

<Steps>

1. **Locking disc:** on the top face of the driver disk, draw a circle on the origin with radius `Spreadsheet.LockRadius` (30 mm). **Pad** it by `Spreadsheet.WheelThickness`.

2. **Cut-away:** on the top face of the locking disc, draw a circle whose center is on the horizontal axis, on the pin side, at `Spreadsheet.DriverCenterDistance` (80 mm) from the origin. Give it the radius `Spreadsheet.ReliefRadius` (70.78 mm). **Pocket** it, Length `Spreadsheet.WheelThickness`. This removes the part of the locking disc that faces the wheel while the pin drives it.

3. **Pin:** on **XY_Plane**, draw a circle at the pin position (horizontal constraint with the origin, distance `Spreadsheet.DriverRadius`) with radius `Spreadsheet.PinRadius`. **Pad** it by `2 * Spreadsheet.WheelThickness` (30 mm). It fills the pin hole and stands 15 mm above the driver disk.

</Steps>

The cut-away must face the same way as the pin. Its edge meets the locking disc 61.5° each side of the pin, a little more than the 60° each side in which the pin drives the wheel.

### Drawing the Geneva Wheel Profile

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Base Circle">

    **Draw the main wheel circle:**

    <Steps>
    1. **Circle tool** (press G, C)
    2. Click at **origin**
    3. Move mouse outward and click
    4. Press **Escape**

    5. **Parametric radius (the calculated one!):**
       - **Radius constraint** tool
       - Click the circle
       - Click **ƒx** button
       - Type: `Spreadsheet.DrivenRadius`
       - Press **Enter**
    </Steps>

    This radius was **automatically calculated** from the Geneva formula!

    :::note[Verify Calculation]
    For 6 slots with DriverRadius = 40mm:
    - DrivenRadius should show approximately 69mm
    - This is $R_d / \tan(30°) \approx 40 / 0.577 \approx 69 \text{ mm}$
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Shaft Hole">

    **Add the center shaft hole:**

    <Steps>
    1. **Circle tool**
    2. Click at **origin**
    3. Draw smaller circle inside
    4. Press **Escape**

    5. **Parametric radius:**
       - **Radius constraint**
       - Click inner circle
       - Click **ƒx** button
       - Type: `Spreadsheet.ShaftRadius`
       - Press **Enter**
    </Steps>

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="First Slot">

    **Do not draw the slot in this sketch.** A slot rectangle that crosses the outer circle makes wires that cross. FreeCAD 1.1 pads such a sketch without a message, but the solid fails FreeCAD's validity check, and later features on it can fail. The slot is cut after the pad (see "Cutting the Slots" under the Pad step below). This sketch keeps only the two circles.

    :::tip[Slot Geometry Tips]
    - Slot must be **radial** (pointing toward center)
    - Slot must **extend beyond** wheel periphery to ensure clean cutout
    - Width controlled by `SlotWidth` parameter (has clearance for pin)
    - Its inner end must stay clear of the pin's deepest point, $C - R_d$ from the wheel center, less the pin radius
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Polar Pattern">

    The polar pattern copies the slot round the wheel. It is made in Part Design after the slot pocket (see "Cutting the Slots" below), not with the Sketcher polar pattern.

    :::caution[Pattern Limitation]
    The Sketcher polar pattern does not accept a spreadsheet formula for the count. The Part Design **Polar Pattern** does: its Occurrences field takes the formula `Spreadsheet.NumSlots` (click **ƒx** beside the field, or type `=`), so a change of NumSlots changes the number of slots.
    :::

</TabItem>

    **Check the sketch:**

    You should now see:
    - Outer circle (Geneva wheel periphery)
    - Inner circle (shaft hole)

    **Verify constraints:**
    - Both radii linked to the spreadsheet
    - Solver shows "Fully constrained"

    **The locking surface:**
    - The rim between the slots will carry the hollows, cut after the pad
    - The driver's locking disc sits in a hollow during the dwell

</TabItem>

<Steps>

**Cutting the Slots:**

1. **Slot sketch** on the top face of the wheel: a rectangle on the horizontal axis to the right of the center. Constrain it with the vertical distance from its bottom left corner to the origin, `Spreadsheet.SlotWidth / 2`; its height, `Spreadsheet.SlotWidth`; its inner end from the origin, `Spreadsheet.DriverCenterDistance - Spreadsheet.DriverRadius - Spreadsheet.PinRadius - 2`; its outer end, `Spreadsheet.DrivenRadius + 5`, beyond the rim. (Do not make two edges symmetric about the axis: that repeats a rule of the Rectangle tool, and the solver reports it redundant.)
2. **Pocket**, Type **Through all**.
3. **Polar Pattern** (Part Design) of the pocket, about the sketch's normal axis, Angle 360°, Occurrences `Spreadsheet.NumSlots`.

The inner end is 33 mm for 6 slots: the pin center comes to $80 - 40 = 40$ mm from the wheel center, so its edge to 35 mm, 2 mm short of the slot end. A typed 25 mm works for 6 slots but not for 4: there the pin edge comes to 18.4 mm and strikes the end of a 25 mm slot. The formula gives 16.4 mm for 4 slots and 42.4 mm for 8.

Rotate the view to see the radial slots cutting through the wheel.

### Cutting the Hollows for the Locking Disc

<Steps>

1. **Sketch on the top face** of the wheel.

2. **First hollow:** draw a circle with its center at `Spreadsheet.DriverCenterDistance` (80 mm) from the origin, on the line halfway between two slots (30° from a slot for 6 slots). Radius: `Spreadsheet.HollowRadius` (30.5 mm). In the dwell the driver's center sits exactly at this point, so the locking disc fits the hollow with 0.5 mm all round.

3. **Pocket**, Type **Through all**.

4. **Polar Pattern** (Part Design) of this pocket, Occurrences `Spreadsheet.NumSlots`, as for the slots.

</Steps>

Each hollow cuts a concave arc into the rim between two slots. Checked over a full driver turn for 4, 6 and 8 slots, with these formulas the locking disc and the pin never touch the wheel, and in the dwell the wheel cannot turn more than about 0.4° (0.5 mm of clearance at 80 mm); for 4 slots the play is a little larger, under 1°.

**Check it:** the wheel is one solid with NumSlots slots and NumSlots hollows. Set NumSlots to 4 and recompute: four slots and four hollows appear, with no pattern to repeat by hand. In FreeCAD 1.1 a full driver turn in 1° steps gave no contact for 4, 6 and 8 slots; in the dwell, turning the wheel 1° by hand made the locking disc cut into it, which is the lock working. A wheel that turns freely in the dwell has its hollows on the slot lines, not halfway between.

## Part 6: Creating the Base Frame

<InArticleAd />


### Design Intent

The frame provides the fixed reference for both rotating wheels:

<Card title="📐 Frame Requirements" icon="setting">

A stationary base with two shaft bearings (driver and Geneva wheel) at the center distance set by `DriverCenterDistance`, and bolt holes to fix it to the machine.

</Card>

### Creating the Frame

<Steps>

1. **Create new Body**

   - Part Design workbench, **New Body**, rename it `Frame`

2. **Create Sketch**

   - Select Frame body, **New Sketch** → XY_Plane

3. **Draw base plate**

   - **Rectangle tool**
   - Draw centered rectangle
   - Width: `200` mm
   - Height: `120` mm
   - Center it with one **Symmetric** constraint: bottom left corner, top right corner, then the origin

4. **Add driver shaft hole**

   - **Circle** at origin
   - Radius: `12` mm (shaft radius + clearance)

5. **Add Geneva wheel shaft hole**

   - **Circle** to the right of origin
   - **Horizontal constraint** with origin
   - **Distance constraint** from origin: Click **ƒx** → `Spreadsheet.DriverCenterDistance`
   - Radius: `12` mm

6. **Add mounting holes (optional)**

   - Four circles at corners
   - Radius: `6` mm (for M10 bolts)
   - Position symmetrically

7. **Close and Pad**

   - Close sketch
   - **Pad**: `20` mm

</Steps>

All three parts are now ready for assembly.

## Part 7: Assembly

<InArticleAd />


In the assembly you see the indexing: the Geneva wheel steps from position to position and stands still between steps.

### Assembly Strategy

<Card title="🎯 Assembly Constraints Plan" icon="rocket">

1. **Frame:** grounded (ground reference)
2. **Driver:** Rotates continuously about left shaft
3. **Geneva Wheel:** Rotates intermittently about right shaft
4. **Pin engagement:** Driver pin enters Geneva slots to create indexing

</Card>

### Creating the Assembly

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Setup">

    <Steps>

    1. **Switch to Assembly workbench**

       Use workbench dropdown

    2. **Create new assembly**

       Assembly → **New Assembly** (the Assembly workbench built into FreeCAD 1.0 and later; in a 0.21 add-on a Revolute joint is called Axial Alignment or axisCoincident)

    3. **Add parts**

       **Insert Component** (**I**):
       - Frame
       - Driver
       - GenevaWheel

    </Steps>

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Fixing Frame">

    <Steps>

    1. Select **Frame** in assembly tree

    2. Click **Toggle Grounded** (**G**)

    3. Frame is now locked in place (ground reference)

    </Steps>

    :::note[Why Fix the Frame?]
    Every mechanism needs a ground reference. The frame provides the fixed mounting from which we measure all rotational motion.
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Driver Rotation">

    **Allow driver to rotate about its shaft:**

    <Steps>

    1. **Revolute Joint** (**R**)

    2. Pick the driver shaft hole edge on the front face of the frame, then the shaft hole edge on the back face of the driver

    </Steps>

    The driver lies on the frame and can spin continuously.

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Geneva Rotation">

    **Allow Geneva wheel to rotate about its shaft:**

    <Steps>

    1. **Revolute Joint**: the right shaft hole edge on the front face of the frame, then the shaft hole edge on the back face of the wheel

    2. In the joint panel set **Offset** to `15` mm (WheelThickness): the wheel then sits at the level of the locking disc, above the driver disk. With 0 it sits on the frame beside the driver disk, and the two overlap

    </Steps>

    The Geneva wheel can now rotate about its axis.

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Initial Position">

    **Position for first index:**

    Manually drag parts so that:
    - The Geneva wheel sits at the height of the **locking disc**, above the driver disk
    - **Driver pin** is entering one of the **Geneva wheel slots**
    - This is the starting position for indexing motion

    :::tip[Alignment Tip]
    Rotate the driver so the pin is just beginning to engage a slot. This is the clearest starting position to see the indexing action!
    :::

</TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Testing Motion">

    **Test the intermittent motion:**

    Try manually rotating the driver in the assembly view:

    1. **Dwell:** the driver turns, the pin is out of the slots, and the Geneva wheel stays **stationary**.
    2. **Index:** the pin enters a slot and the Geneva wheel turns 60°.
    3. **Return to dwell:** the pin leaves the slot and the wheel stops in its new position.

    Repeat for all 6 positions.

    :::tip[Troubleshooting Motion]
    **If mechanism won't move properly:**
    - Check that only Frame is grounded
    - Both wheels should be Revolute joints (not Fixed)
    - Verify center distance is correct
    - Ensure pin can physically enter slots
    - Check for collision interference
    :::

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Mathematical Background

<InArticleAd />


### Geneva Wheel Radius Calculation

For an external Geneva mechanism:

$$R_g = \frac{R_d}{\tan\left(\frac{180°}{n}\right)}$$

Where:
- $R_g$ = Geneva wheel radius (center to slot entry)
- $R_d$ = Driver radius (center to pin)
- $n$ = Number of slots

**For n = 6:**

$$R_g = \frac{R_d}{\tan(30°)}$$

$$R_g = \frac{R_d}{0.577} = 1.732 \times R_d$$

If $R_d = 40 \text{ mm}$, then $R_g \approx 69 \text{ mm}$.

### Dwell vs. Index Time

**Dwell angle:**

$$\text{Dwell} = 180° + \frac{360°}{n} = \frac{360° \times (n + 2)}{2n}$$

**Index angle:**

$$\text{Index} = 180° - \frac{360°}{n}$$

**For 6-slot Geneva:**
- Dwell: $180° + 60° = 240°$ of driver rotation
- Index: $180° - 60° = 120°$ of driver rotation (the wheel itself turns $360°/6 = 60°$)

## Common Issues and Solutions

<InArticleAd />


### ``Slots not evenly spaced"

- **Cause:** Polar pattern not set to correct angle or count
- **Solution:** Verify SlotAngle = 360 / NumSlots
- **Fix:** Check pattern constraints reference spreadsheet parameters

### ``Pin doesn't fit in slot"

- **Cause:** Slot width too narrow
- **Solution:** SlotWidth should be 1.2-1.5 × PinDiameter
- **Fix:** Adjust SlotWidth parameter

### ``Geneva wheel radius seems wrong"

- **Cause:** Incorrect tan formula or units
- **Solution:** Verify formula: `=B8/tan(180/B2)`
- **Fix:** Check that the angle is in degrees (write `180 deg / B2` to be explicit)

### ``Mechanism binds during motion"

- **Cause:** Center distance incorrect or slot angle wrong
- **Solution:** Recalculate center distance using correct geometry
- **Fix:** Verify all geometric relationships
